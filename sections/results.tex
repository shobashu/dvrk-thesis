\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results below come from bench trials run by the author on the
physical dVRK, used to validate that the pipeline works as described
in Section~\ref{sec:methods}, not from a study with trainees; that
study is future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}). Rather than
validating each measure in isolation, this chapter follows the same
logic as the pipeline itself (Section~\ref{subsec:pipeline_overview}):
the measures are merged into one system, so results are shown as what
one trial, run through the whole pipeline at once, actually produces,
with timing broken out on its own, since it calls for a direct,
quantitative check against the real-time claim this thesis makes.

\subsection{Object Detection}
\label{subsec:results_detection}

Every measure in Table~\ref{tab:data_timing} depends on the YOLOv8
detector locating the cylinder correctly first, so its accuracy is
reported on its own rather than only inferred from the pipeline
behaving well end to end.

The detector (Section~\ref{subsec:object_detection}) was evaluated on
a held-out test split of 85 frames, 82 of them containing the
cylinder, drawn from the same recording sessions as training.
Table~\ref{tab:detector_performance} summarizes the result.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\hline
Metric & Value \\
\hline
Precision & 0.996 \\
Recall & 1.000 \\
mAP50 & 0.995 \\
mAP50-95 & 0.783 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{YOLOv8 cylinder detector performance on the held-out test
split (85 images, 82 instances).}
\label{tab:detector_performance}
\end{table}

The gap between mAP50 and mAP50-95 indicates that while the detector
rarely misses or misfires on the cylinder, its boxes are not always
tightly localized; since the orientation check
(Section~\ref{subsec:angular_displacement}) segments color directly
from this box, looser boxes are a more likely source of orientation
error than missed detections.

\subsection{Object State Classifier}
\label{subsec:results_state_classifier}

The XGBoost classifier (Section~\ref{subsec:object_state}) was
trained on 13{,}098 labeled frames pooled from three sessions
(4{,}046, 4{,}808, and 4{,}256 rows each before cleaning), covering
73 stationary, 75 held, 44 dropped, and 5 lost events. Lost events
were kept deliberately few: unlike the other three states, lost does
not need to be learned from examples, since it is directly recoverable
procedurally whenever the detector reports nothing for several
consecutive frames (Section~\ref{subsec:object_state}). Because
cross-validation is grouped by session (Section~\ref{subsec:object_state}),
its 3-fold accuracy and F1-macro (Table~\ref{tab:xgb_cv_summary}) are
a rough signal, not a precise estimate: each fold trains on two
sessions and validates on a third the model has not seen any part of.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\hline
Metric & Value (mean $\pm$ std) \\
\hline
Accuracy & 0.893 $\pm$ 0.049 \\
F1-macro & 0.722 $\pm$ 0.111 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{3-fold, leave-one-session-out cross-validation summary for
the object-state classifier.}
\label{tab:xgb_cv_summary}
\end{table}

The pooled out-of-fold predictions (Figure~\ref{fig:xgb_oof_metrics})
give the genuine per-class generalization estimate, as opposed to the
near-100\% scores the same model gets when evaluated on the data it
was trained on. Stationary and held, the two most common states, are
classified reliably (F1 0.92 and 0.90). Dropped is weaker (F1 0.72,
recall 0.64), and lost is the least reliable of the four (F1 0.46,
recall 0.30). This is expected rather than a data-scarcity problem to
fix: as noted above, lost was deliberately underrepresented in
training because it is not something the classifier needs to learn,
being directly recoverable from the detector's own miss count. The
confusion matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:xgb_oof_confusion})
shows most of that error going one direction, lost frames misread as
held (216 of 308), rather than spread evenly across the other states.

The top feature importances (Figure~\ref{fig:xgb_feature_importance}) are
dominated by lagged pixel velocity (\texttt{vel\_px\_s\_lag1-4}),
consistent with state depending on recent motion history rather than
any single frame's position; PSM1's $z$-position and orientation, and
the cylinder's own bounding-box position, follow behind velocity as
secondary signals.
